DESIGN REVIEW · 10 AUG 2026

August Attack
What I'd change

attack.urbanpadel.om 3 SCREENS REVIEWED TARGET: 430×932 VOL.6 · 9 SESSIONS
THE CORE PROBLEM

It's built like a poster.
It's used like a scoreboard.

The visual language is genuinely good — square plates, corner ticks, Anton over mono numerals, red leading cyan. Nothing here needs a restyle.

What it needs is a change of job. A player opens this between games with one of two questions: when do I play next, and where do I stand. Right now the first viewport of HOME answers neither — it spends all 932 points on branding, and the answers sit 800 and 3,400 points down. Everything below follows from that.

01
BLOCKER HOME

The first screen is two logos and a tagline

The header mark and the hero mark are the same lockup within 700 points of each other. Below them: "Urban Playground presents", the Attack lockup, "Serve first. Strike hard.", a schedule chip. Then the fold ends. The tagline appears three times on the page.

FIX

Compress the hero to a 96pt band — Attack lockup, dates, nothing else. Drop the duplicate mark from the hero and keep it in the header. One tagline, in the band. The poster already exists as the IG carousel; the app doesn't need to repeat it every visit.

02
BLOCKER HOME · SESSIONS

"Next up" is showing a session marked done

HOME's NEXT UP card is Session 2 with a green DONE badge. SESSIONS lists two of the nine, both DONE, then 1,200 points of empty black. A nine-session series is presenting itself as finished, and the one question players actually came with has no answer anywhere in the app.

FIX

Publish all nine session slots up front with three states — done, next, scheduled. NEXT UP points at the first non-done slot and carries date, time, countdown and signup state. The empty screen becomes a schedule, and the series reads as 2 of 9 rather than over.

03
HIGH ALL SCREENS

There is no "me" anywhere

Points follow the player, not the team — that's the whole hook of the format. Yet nothing in the app knows who is holding the phone. Every player gets the same broadcast, and rank 33 scrolls past 32 strangers to find their own row.

FIX

One tap: pick your name from the 48, store it locally, no account. HOME then leads with your rank, points, movement and gap to the next place. RANK pins your row to the top of the list. Cheap to build, and it converts a noticeboard into a personal tracker.

04
HIGH HOME

Half of HOME is a rulebook

HOW TO SCORE runs roughly 1,500 points: five group rows, four knockout rows, the streak ladder, comeback bonus, the 2× rule, loyalty lockout. It's well made and it is read once. It sits permanently between the standings and the bottom of the page.

FIX

Move it behind the FORMAT sheet that already exists on SESSIONS, and leave a one-line "How points work →" on HOME. Keep only the live parts inline — the 2× flag on sessions 8 and 9 belongs on those session cards, where it changes a decision.

05
HIGH RANK

48 rows, all shouting equally

4,400 points with no search, no filter, no jump. Every row carries red corner ticks, a photo, a five-game strip and a large red total — including ranks 39–48, who sit on 0–3 points with grey pills. When the accent is on everything it marks nothing, and the tail dilutes the top.

FIX

Search at the top, your row pinned under it. Corner ticks and red totals reserved for the top three; ranks 4+ get a thin mono row. Collapse everyone on 3 points or fewer behind "Show 12 more". The list drops to about a third of its height and the podium means something again.

06
MEDIUM RANK · SESSIONS

The palette is losing its own argument

RANK reads cyan-first: a cyan title, a cyan crown, a cyan count chip. The heaviest object on the screen is a striped dull-green banner about the biggest climber, in a system where green means live. SESSIONS' loudest element is a blue number chip left over from July Heat, and the initials avatars run blue, purple, orange and teal — none of them in the palette.

FIX

Title in chalk, count in steel, cyan back to a supporting voice. Climber becomes a one-line note under the podium, not a banner. Convert the six blue chips and seven off-green values, and render initials avatars as steel on Court Black — red only inside the top ten. That restores the 3:1 ratio without touching layout.

07
MEDIUM SESSIONS

Admin controls sit above player content

ANNOUNCE, SIGNUP and FORMAT are the first thing on SESSIONS, styled as the primary actions of the screen. Two of the three are yours, not the players'. There's also a back arrow at the top of a bottom-nav tab, which has nothing to go back to.

FIX

Admin actions into a single control behind the existing admin token; FORMAT stays visible as a quiet link since it's for everyone. Delete the back arrow on tab roots.

08
MEDIUM DELIVERY

Half the payload is one logo, three times

The same 0.41MB PNG is base64'd three times inside a 1.67MB file. Players load this on venue wifi between games, and the page shows the word LOADING until React, Tailwind and Babel have all arrived and compiled. Rajdhani is referenced in three rules and never loaded, so those rules quietly render as Archivo.

FIX

Reference the embedded logo once and reuse it — that alone removes about 0.8MB. Delete the Rajdhani rules. Then replace LOADING with the header band and skeleton rows in plain HTML, so the first paint is branded rather than blank. All three are inside the single-file constraint.

IF YOU ONLY DO THREE THINGS
  1. Put the next session and a working signup at the top of HOME, and publish all nine slots.
  2. Let a player say who they are, once, and lead with their standing.
  3. Add search to RANK and thin the rows below third place.
WORTH SAYING

None of this asks for a toolchain, a rename of any storage key, or an edit to an existing style block. The identity, the plates and the corner-tick motif all stay exactly as they are — they're the strongest part of the product. The changes are about what occupies the top of each screen, and how much of the screen each fact is allowed to take.
